## P0 — Agree on the first study and design

**Deliverable:** a short study brief and clickable or static screen flow, reviewed before application scaffolding.

1. Choose one decision the study should inform, the participant profile and the interview format.
2. Draft a real guide with 5–8 core questions, optional probes and one screener/branch.
3. Design the researcher guide editor, participant interview and response-to-evidence drill-down.
4. Set a pilot budget, provider choices and an initial target for interview duration.
5. Decide completion-cap behavior and the distinction between publishing a guide and opening fielding.

**Done when:** we can walk through both researcher and participant journeys and agree on what a successful finding looks like. P0 is a design decision, not a claim that research has run.

## P1 — Complete the workflow in text

**Delivers F01–F09, F13, F18, F21 and the basic permission/data foundations of F39/F43.**

Build in this order:

1. **P1.1 Foundation:** app shell, authenticated researcher, workspace-scoped persistence, study list and study state. Participant session tokens are scoped to a single interview.
2. **P1.2 Guide:** objectives, audience, sections, open-ended/choice/statement questions, stable IDs, simple screener and earlier-answer branches. Manual editing remains authoritative.
3. **P1.3 AI draft:** generate a structured guide from the brief. Validate output and let the researcher review changes before saving; rejected/invalid output never silently changes the guide.
4. **P1.4 Preview and publish:** preview without polluting real responses; save an immutable guide revision; separate published revision from fielding state.
5. **P1.5 Interview:** consent, screener, transcript, bounded adaptive probes, persisted progress, reconnect/resume, completion and screen-out reasons.
6. **P1.6 Fielding and review:** participant link, pause/close, admission limit, response grid, full transcripts and manual exclude/restore.

**Acceptance:** a researcher creates and publishes a guide; an invited participant qualifies, follows the expected branch, receives a relevant bounded probe and finishes. Another participant screens out. A refreshed session resumes without duplicate turns. Closed fielding rejects new sessions. Preview sessions never enter production counts. Active interviews retain their original guide revision after edits. One workspace cannot read another workspace's data.

**Review together:** run the same guide ourselves, inspect every probe and adjust the interview rules before connecting voice.

## P2 — Make voice interviewing usable

**Delivers voice in F04/F18, audio review in F21 and pilot access/operations.**

1. Add microphone preflight, voice input/output, clear recording state and text fallback. Decide voice turn-taking through a short provider spike; target natural exchanges and interruption support.
2. Preserve stable turn IDs and source timestamps across audio, transcript and question IDs. Support recoverable chunk uploads and safe retries.
3. Handle denied permissions, dropped connections, slow responses, transcription failures and participant stop requests. Save completed work before ending a failed session.
4. Give team members appropriate study access and restrict launch to authorized researchers. Add operational logs and per-interview model/audio cost accounting.
5. Implement consent records, retention configuration, deletion across transcripts/media/derived indexes, and tests for unauthorized recording access before external participants enter.

**Acceptance:** two consenting testers finish voice sessions in supported browsers, including one interruption and one reconnect; transcript links play the correct audio range. Denying the microphone allows text fallback. The researcher can see failed versus complete sessions. Deletion removes raw data and prevents it from appearing in regenerated outputs. Record measured latency and cost; agree on pass targets after the provider spike, before a larger pilot.

**Review together:** listen to full recordings and judge neutrality, probe relevance, pacing and recovery. Do not judge interview quality from a summary alone.

## P3 — Turn responses into defensible findings

**Delivers core F22–F28 and a useful first release.**

1. Generate question-level summaries, proposed themes, representative quotes and notable counterexamples. Let researchers edit coding and exclude/restore responses with reasons.
2. Calculate theme counts, percentages and basic segment comparisons in code. Display eligible N, missing answers and multi-coded responses; don't have an LLM invent arithmetic or significance.
3. Build a source-linked analysis view. Citations resolve to respondent, question, exact transcript span and audio range where available.
4. Add grounded study Q&A. Separate observation from interpretation and say when evidence is insufficient. Retrieved participant text is evidence, not instructions to the analysis agent.
5. Generate an editable memo with objective, sample/method, findings, contrary evidence, limitations and citations. Include CSV export and controlled view-only sharing.
6. Version analysis against the guide revisions, included response set, coding and model/prompt version. Mark stale outputs when underlying data changes. Store timestamp bookmarks now; render reels later.

**Acceptance:** use a small human-reviewed fixture corpus with known themes, a minority viewpoint, incomplete responses and two segments. Counts match recomputed values; every displayed quote matches its source exactly; unsupported questions receive no fabricated answer. Excluding a response changes the denominator and marks prior reports stale; regeneration uses the new set. Share access can be revoked. These fixtures validate behavior; they do not establish real-world model accuracy.

**Pilot:** run one real self-recruited study, with 5–10 interviews as a workflow and quality check. Review all source evidence and record failures/cost. This is a usability pilot, not a representative sample or validation of population estimates.

**Review together:** compare the generated memo with our own read of the interviews. Decide whether the product saves work without weakening the findings.

## P5 — Add specialist research modules

Each module needs its own research specification and validation dataset.

- **Visual Insights (F32):** first generate timestamped screen-event observations for human review. Only then test live screen-aware probes, off-task recovery and say/do comparisons. Verify timing and metrics against annotated recordings; model descriptions cannot automatically establish click counts or task success.
- **Gabor–Granger (F35):** specify ladder/admission rules, nonbuyer denominators, inconsistent responses and incomplete ladders. Verify demand/revenue curves on hand-calculated examples and compare with an accepted implementation.
- **MaxDiff (F34):** specify experimental design, best/worst capture and model estimation. Validate recovery and uncertainty on simulated/known data. Simple best-minus-worst counts must not be labeled equivalent to Listen's documented Hierarchical Bayes scoring. Experimental hierarchical estimation and portfolio comparisons are now implemented in [advanced methods](advanced-research-methods.md).
- **Card sort (F36):** start with defined groups/cards and follow-up reasoning. Verify group assignments and aggregate results. Open/hybrid sorting is now implemented in [advanced methods](advanced-research-methods.md).
- **Pulse (F37):** version recurring waves, comparable questions/audiences and coding. Demonstrate that design changes are flagged rather than rendered as clean trends. Trend observation alone does not prove predictive performance.
- **Emotional signals (F33):** optional experiment. Label inferred emotion as a hypothesis and show source moments; evaluate across chosen languages/groups before relying on scores. Use self-reported emotion as a practical early alternative.
- **Twins (F38):** separate research proposal only after substantial real interview data. Define held-out prediction tests, uncertainty calibration and abstention before claiming predictive value. Synthetic answers must stay separate from observed participant data.

## Proposed technical direction

These are starting recommendations, pending P0. They are not deductions about Listen's stack.

- TypeScript web app with Next.js, PostgreSQL, object storage for media and background jobs for transcription, analysis and exports. Keep the first app in one repository; avoid separate services until a concrete need appears.
- Use managed authentication and hosted model/speech APIs. Compare a realtime voice provider with a streaming speech-to-text → model → text-to-speech pipeline in the P2 spike. No provider or paid account has been selected.
- Keep study schema/routing, interview runtime and analysis as distinct modules. Both preview and production use the same deterministic routing engine.
- Minimum entities: Workspace, Membership, Study, StudyRevision, Block/Question/Option, ParticipantSession, Consent, Turn/Answer, Recording, QualityReview, Segment, ThemeAssignment, EvidenceCitation, AnalysisRun and ReportVersion.
- Store model/provider/prompt version and source response IDs with generated artifacts. Compute aggregates from persisted answers/coding. Don't persist hidden model reasoning; keep observable source evidence and concise explanations.
- Keep secrets server-side, protect media access and treat participant identifiers separately from research content. Choose provider retention/training settings explicitly.
